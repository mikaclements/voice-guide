---
name: schematic
description: Update the schematic (`schematic/data/schematic.json`) for a change that adds, changes or removes a service, a package, a port or its methods, an adapter, a connection between parts, a design decision, or what happens in a walk-through. Run in every feature build before verifying, and whenever `go test` fails in `cmd/classgen` on a package or an import the schematic doesn't show.
---

The schematic draws voice-guide level by level: level 1 the system (the page, voice, the other services, PostgreSQL, outside callers), level 2 the parts inside voice (the modules and shared packages), level 3 each module's core, ports and adapters, and level 4 the types in its code, generated by `cmd/classgen` and never written by hand. Levels 1 to 3 are written by hand in `schematic/data/schematic.json`; its `_readme` defines every field. Each of them tells two to four walk-throughs (`flows`), named stories the reader plays step by step; at level 3 the page folds each adapter behind the port it implements (or the core type it calls) until clicked, which needs nothing written. Changes to `schematic/` and `cmd/classgen/` don't count toward a pull request's size.

1. What changed. `git diff --stat origin/main... -- cmd internal services web compose.yaml migrations docs/reference`, and the change's own description. List each service, package, port (an interface in a module's core), adapter, connection (a call between parts, an HTTP or WebSocket route, the SQL a store runs) and design decision that was added, changed or removed, and each whose behaviour a card or a label describes.
2. Update the data at the level that shows each:
   - Level 1: a service, store or outside caller is a card; each connection between them an edge labelled with what is sent and what comes back, and its protocol.
   - Level 2: a module or shared package is a card in the voice box. Every package under `internal/` sits inside one of them, and every import from one part to another needs an edge between them, labelled with what is called. `cmd/voice` is drawn with the calls it makes itself (the idle sweep, the job runner), not its wiring; a package of plain types every part imports is marked `importedFreely`.
   - Level 3: the core's types that hold the rules; each port with its interface's methods as `members` (a line naming another interface the port declares, then a colon, lists that interface's methods, as the speech transport's does for its connection); each adapter tagged inbound or outbound, with an `implements` edge to its port, or for an inbound adapter an edge to the core type it calls, and an edge to what it reaches; what the module talks to as faded context cards. Those edges are what folding reads: an adapter with neither stays shown. A new module gets its own level-3 view, opened from its level-2 card.
   - Level 4: a new module gets a class view (`type: classes`, `source`, `root`), linked from its level-3 view by `classes`; each level-3 card drills to it with `focus` set to its own package. Nothing else to write: `scripts/schematic.sh` generates it.
   - A package deliberately drawn nowhere (a port's fake, test support, a developer's check) goes under `omitted`, with why; the levels show what runs, so fakes are never drawn, and `scripts/schematic.sh` leaves them out of the type views too.
   - Walk-throughs: each level with cards has two to four `flows`, named for what happens ("You say something", "A session ends"), each of three to seven steps. A step names the cards and connections it lights (a connection's two cards always among them) and one plain caption: who does what to whom, in order, as the code does it. A change to what happens (a new call, a step reordered, a limit changed) updates the captions that tell it; a card or connection renamed or removed is renamed or removed in every step.
   - Why: a card that is built a particular way for a reason gets `why`, one plain sentence from the design's reasoning, shown in the panel when it is clicked. Update it when the reason changes.
   - Decisions: each consideration is one design decision from `docs/reference/architecture.md` that a level shows, with `ref` its anchor there; on the page it is a folded link list under a level, a card or a connection, its full title (never its label) linking to the design, with the decision in one plain sentence beneath. Cite it from the views, cards and edges it shapes. Update one whose decision changed; remove one no view, card or edge cites any more.
3. Write it right:
   - Read every label and caption from the code: the adapter's requests, the core's calls, the store's queries. Never guess what flows.
   - The design's terms exactly (its Appendix A), no synonyms. Cards and captions name parts by what they do (the text-to-speech service, the end-of-turn check); a vendor or library goes on a card's `tech` line or in an adapter's own description.
   - A card's summary under about 110 characters; an edge's `send` under about 60. Both are cut on the canvas, and the panel shows the description and the detail in full.
4. Check:
   - `go test ./cmd/classgen/`: every package named and inside a part of voice, every package, file and path named exists, every import between the parts of voice drawn, every port's members the methods of its interface.
   - `npm test --workspace schematic`: every card, view and decision referred to exists, every decision's anchor is still in the design, every walk-through step names cards and connections its level has, every level with cards has a walk-through, and every hand-written level lays out with no card over another, folded, unfolded and with each walk-through's cards shown.
   - `scripts/schematic.sh check` (needs Chrome): screenshots every level into `schematic/.shots/` as it opens (`<level>.png`), with a host card clicked (`-opened`), with every adapter and label shown (`-all`) and at the middle step of each walk-through (`-walk-<flow>`), and fails on overlaps or cut-off text. Look at the screenshots of the levels you touched; fix anything that reads badly.
5. Report the levels changed and what was added to each, the walk-throughs changed, with the check's output.
